Decision Models Become Standard API Category

Decision models are APIs that take structured questions and return structured answers, designed for multi-choice or scoring scenarios without reasoning errors. They're cheaper and more reliable than prompting. Jev pioneered in September; OpenAI and Google followed. Each provider confirms pricing and interface standards.

What changed

Google's entry makes clear this is a category, not a one-off. Third major platform in three weeks confirms standardization.

Key signals
  • ◆Gemini Decision 1 supports yes/no, choice, and rubric-scoring decisions (matches Jev interface)
  • ◆Reuses Jev's term 'Noul' for yes/no questions, priced same as GPT-6 Luna ($0.10/million input tokens for short prompts)
  • ◆OpenAI released decisions API in October; Jev released in September. Google confirms this is a category, not anomaly.
  • ◆Pattern: structured reasoning APIs now expected across tier-1 providers; reduces prompt engineering and hallucination risk for discrete decisions
What this means for you

Your agency automates client decisions (approval workflows, routing, classification). Decision APIs reduce prompt engineering and are cheaper than full model inference for simple tasks. Test Gemini Decision 1 this week for workflows currently using multi-turn reasoning, especially where clients audit decision logic.

AI Agents Hit Real-World Liability and ToS Constraints

AI agents can now execute API calls autonomously. United's ban reflects real operational and liability concerns: agents making wrong bookings, creating customer support overhead, bypassing platform pricing controls. This is the first explicit carrier ban, but expect similar restrictions on Expedia, Kayak, and other ticketing platforms.

What changed

United formalized the ban this week after agents reportedly made unwanted bookings. This moves from anecdotal friction to written policy.

Key signals
  • ◆Prohibition explicitly targets agents making bookings without explicit per-transaction approval
  • ◆Reflects liability and customer support friction from agent-made reservations
  • ◆First major carrier to formalize agent booking ban; likely to spread to other airlines, ticketing platforms
  • ◆Signals platform operators are drawing hard lines around autonomous purchasing
What this means for you

Your clients will hit ToS friction when deploying agents. High-stakes transactions (bookings, payments) increasingly blocked by platforms. Design agent workflows that ask for explicit approval before external API calls, or build client processes that stay inside their own systems (approvals, routing, extraction) without external automation.
