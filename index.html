Understanding LLM outputs becomes the bottleneck; prompt engineering is the easy part.

💡 Learn
07
Story thread · new
Karpathy: We'll spend far more time understanding LLM outputs than tuning prompts

Karpathy is tracking how practitioners actually use frontier models. This tweet is based on hands-on experience with Opus, Claude, and GPT models at scale. The pattern: as models get better, the constraint moves from model capability to human ability to verify and control outputs.

What changed

Karpathy publicly signals that interpretability and output validation are now higher-leverage than prompt tuning.

Key signals
  • ◆Shift in focus: From 'what do I ask the model' to 'what is the model actually doing when it responds'
  • ◆Controlled language for clarity: Testing ASD-STE100 (aviation standard) to get LLMs to explain in constraint-driven specs; works better than natural language for debugging
  • ◆Voice ramble sessions: When lazy, switch to voice input and ramble—LLM uses extra context to infer intent better than a typed prompt
What this means for you

This is a workflow shift: your agency should invest in interpretability tools and LLM output validation before investing in prompt engineering. Build client workflows that include human review checkpoints for LLM outputs, not just automation. Test ASD-STE100 prompts for client-facing automations that require clarity.

Recommended action

test, adopt ASD-STE100 or similar controlled-language prompting for client workflows that require explainability.

Why this made the cut: Signals workflow inversion: interpretation > tuning; actionable for agency practice.
