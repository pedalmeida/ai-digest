Story thread · developing
Understanding LLM outputs and steering is now the constraint

Karpathy signals a paradigm shift: raw model capability is no longer the blocker. Understanding what models produce—filtering hallucinations, validating reasoning, extracting reliable outputs—is now the hard problem. This has deep implications for how to design agentic systems and production workflows.

What changed

Building on his April tweet about capability gaps widening; now offering concrete techniques (controlled languages, voice sessions, prompt anthropometry).

Key signals
  • ◆Prompt engineering is easy; understanding what models actually produce is hard. Gap widening between those who know LLM capability boundaries and those who don't
  • ◆ASD-STE100 (controlled language) helps models explain reasoning more clearly; reduces misinterpretation of outputs
  • ◆Voice-based rambling sessions work better than typed prompts when context is complex; model needs 'more bits' to understand intent
  • ◆Opus 5.5 can handle 1M token budgets (~$10) for complex synthesis tasks; capability ceiling keeps rising but interpretability ceiling is static
What this means for you

For your Portuguese SMB agency, this is your edge: most competitors chase capability upgrades; you should invest in output validation, controlled-language prompts, and reasoning-trace extraction. Building workflows that interpret model outputs reliably (not just prompt better) will differentiate your agency from commodity AI consultants. Start with ASD-STE100 techniques in your next client engagement.

Story thread · new
Minos: physical lab environment for agent evaluation

Leash Bio has built a reproducible scientific evaluation framework. They're releasing Minos—dozens of pre-measured chemistry environments plus agent transcripts—so the community can study how models improve on constrained, measurable domains. This is the first major open-weights agent benchmark with physical ground truth.

What changed

New release; Minos framework and datasets now available to community.

Key signals
  • ◆Agents weren't ready to do medicinal chemistry a year ago. Now models like Opus 5.5 show measurable progress on large decision-space tasks
  • ◆Leash measuring thousands of proteins against millions of molecules in physical lab, then releasing transcripts and decision traces publicly
  • ◆Lab-in-the-loop with zero latency; agents can iterate on real molecular outcomes without waiting for wet lab feedback
  • ◆Agents make systematic mistakes in chemistry; Leash quantifies them; community can learn what models are bad at
What this means for you

Not directly actionable for your Portuguese SMB agency, but important pattern: agents need structured evaluation environments to improve. If you're building automation workflows for clients (e.g., supplier matching, process optimization), creating equivalent 'measurement worlds' (ground-truth datasets, decision traces) will improve your agent reliability and differentiate your work from off-the-shelf prompting.
