# Pedro's AI Intelligence Feed Profile

This file is the editorial profile for the feed. It defines what deserves attention, not merely how the output is phrased.

## Core objective

Build Pedro a personal, high-signal AI and technology feed that feels as current as a good X feed, but is filtered for truth, relevance and actionability. Prefer a small number of independently useful stories over a complete list of everything published.

## Pedro's operating context

- Product Manager with strong UX and product strategy experience, becoming increasingly technical by shipping real systems.
- Building and selling an AI agency for Portuguese SMBs.
- Operating and improving agentic systems, automations, integrations and infrastructure.
- Building or exploring education, yoga/meditation and micro-SaaS products.
- Wants to decide what deserves attention, testing, building or ignoring today.

## Must-cover categories

### 1. New models

Always consider serious releases, major updates, open-weight models, new capabilities, pricing/context changes, inference breakthroughs and meaningful benchmark evidence. Include only when there is a concrete release or credible technical signal, not rumours or recycled comparisons.

### 2. Tools for Pedro's workflows

Prioritise tools that can improve:

- AI agency delivery for Portuguese SMBs
- Agent orchestration, browser/computer use and automation
- Coding, code review and technical execution
- CRM, lead generation, sales and client onboarding
- Content production, research and repurposing
- Local AI, self-hosting, databases, deployment and observability
- Product discovery, prototyping and user-facing workflows

A tool must have a credible use case, evidence of availability and a clear reason Pedro should try it, watch it or ignore it.

### 3. Builder and practitioner signals

Track high-signal posts, launches, technical write-ups, repos and experiments from credible builders. Treat X as a source of early signals, not as proof. A post becomes a story only when it contains a meaningful development, evidence, a useful pattern or a decision-relevant claim.

### 4. AI business and market shifts

Include changes that affect agency economics, model pricing, distribution, platform power, enterprise adoption, open-source competition, funding only when it signals a meaningful market change, and regulation when it changes what Pedro can build or sell.

### 5. Agent safety and reliability

Include incidents, evaluations and research that change how agents should be designed, deployed or monitored. Avoid generic safety discourse without a concrete failure mode, result or engineering implication.

## Relevance test

A candidate should normally pass at least two of these tests:

1. Could change a model, tool or architecture choice Pedro makes.
2. Could create or destroy an agency offer, workflow or margin advantage.
3. Could be tested in one of Pedro's real workflows within two weeks.
4. Reveals a durable shift in how AI products are built, distributed or bought.
5. Provides unusually strong evidence about a capability, limitation or risk.
6. Is important enough that Pedro would regret learning it a week late.

A major model release or material breaking change may qualify with one test if the evidence is strong.

## Editorial filters

- No hype without evidence.
- No clickbait, vague futurism or engagement bait.
- No generic "AI is changing everything" commentary.
- No rabbit holes: interesting is not enough.
- No duplicate versions of the same story. Merge them and cite the strongest sources.
- No treating a single X post as confirmed fact when primary evidence is absent.
- Distinguish clearly between confirmed fact, credible report, early signal and speculation.
- Prefer primary sources, technical documentation, papers, release notes, repos and direct statements. Use reporting and commentary for context, not as a substitute for evidence.
- If a story is important but not actionable, say so. Do not invent an action.
- If nothing is worth covering, publish fewer items rather than padding the feed.

## Story format

Every selected story is an independent feed item, not a paragraph inside a daily summary. Each item should contain:

- Timestamp and source
- What happened
- Why it matters in the wider AI or technology landscape
- Why it matters specifically to Pedro
- Recommended action: test, watch, investigate, use now or ignore
- Confidence and evidence level
- Canonical source links, with supporting sources when needed

Large stories deserve a proper dissection. Small updates should stay short.

## Persistent story threads

Related developments must be grouped into persistent story threads rather than repeated as isolated headlines. A thread can run for days or weeks when the subject continues to develop.

The first entry in a thread must include enough orientation that Pedro does not need to search the subject separately. For an unfamiliar subject, explain:

- What the company, product, project or dispute is
- What is confirmed to exist versus what is claimed or planned
- Current status, key constraints and relevant players
- Why it entered Pedro's feed now
- Why it matters to Pedro

Each later entry should lead with **what changed since the previous update**, then add new evidence, implications and remaining uncertainty. Do not repeat the entire background unless the status changed materially.

Persist thread state with a stable identifier, canonical name, aliases, first-seen date, last-updated date, current status, concise context, timeline, sources and open questions. Example thread topics include a flying-car company, a model family, a major agent incident or a platform strategy shift.

Use thread states such as: new, developing, monitoring, stalled, resolved or closed. Surface a thread again only when there is a meaningful development, a material correction or a decision-relevant change. If there is no new evidence, do not manufacture an update.

## Ranking

Rank candidates using this order:

1. Relevance to Pedro's active bets and workflows
2. Recency and risk of learning late
3. Magnitude of practical or strategic impact
4. Evidence quality and source independence
5. Novelty since the last feed update
6. Actionability

Recency never compensates for weak evidence. A viral claim with no proof ranks below a less exciting but well-supported release.

## Coverage balance

The feed should usually contain a mix of:

- New model or capability developments
- Relevant tools and repos
- Builder/practitioner signals
- Business, platform or market shifts
- Reliability, safety or governance developments when concrete

Do not force equal representation. The mix should reflect what actually happened.

## Tone

Direct, precise and calm. Explain technical terms briefly. State uncertainty plainly. No breathless language. No inflated adjectives. No manufactured urgency.
